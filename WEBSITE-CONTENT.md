## Our Mission (/why)

### Hero
- Eyebrow: Why Opolo Exists
- Headline: Neurodiverse learners face a specific and solvable problem.
- Subhead: Assignments pile up without context. Priorities are unclear. Starting feels impossible. And somewhere along the way, needing help starts to feel like a personal failure. Opolo was built to solve the practical problem first, and in doing so, remove the emotional weight that comes with it.
- Mission pull quote: Our mission at Opolo is ambitious, to help neurodiverse learners build the confidence to navigate their schoolwork on their own, in their own way, and on their own terms.

### Who Opolo Is For
- Eyebrow: Who Opolo Is For
- Headline: Built for the moment schoolwork becomes more complex.
- Deck: Opolo is built for secondary school students with ADHD and executive functioning challenges to start, with a wider range of neurodiverse learners to follow.
- Cards:
  - Card 1 badge: Primary Users
  - Card 1 title: Middle school students
  - Card 1 body: The shift from elementary school brings six teachers, denser assignments, and a system that no longer scales with a neurodiverse learner.
  - Card 2 badge: Additional Support
  - Card 2 title: High school students
  - Card 2 body: For neurodiverse students who need an extra layer of scaffolding as the work grows and the stakes climb.
- Anchor sentence: The support that worked in elementary school does not scale with middle school. For neurodiverse learners, that gap is where things fall apart.

### The Real Problem We Are Solving
- Eyebrow: The Real Problem We Are Solving
- Headline: Opolo is not solely about academic outcomes.
- Body: It is about reducing the emotional burden that comes with being a kid who needs extra support, so they can show up, be present, and learn. When neurodiverse students carry shame and anxiety about needing help, none of the support reaches them. Our goal is that Opolo gives students agency over their own learning and in time builds confidence.
- Pull quote: We believe academic outcomes follow from that. But they are not the starting point.

### A Note from the Founder (expandable)
- Toggle label: A Note from the Founder
- Paragraph 1: My oldest son had a happy, supported start in a small preschool where he thrived. When he transitioned into the larger public school system, it was like a different kid emerged overnight. Every week brought another call or email from his teacher. One I cannot forget said he did not belong in kindergarten yet, that he was too immature.
- Paragraph 2: His ADHD diagnosis came not long after, and it was bittersweet. We finally had an answer, and I believed that meant we could fix it. What I did not understand until much later, when I received my own ADHD diagnosis, was that there was never anything to fix. I threw everything at the problem anyway. IEP, 504 plan, medication, tutors. In hindsight those services were designed to make his time at school more equitable, not to help him build the habits and skills to navigate the work on his own. As a parent it felt like doing something, but the real gap was never addressed.
- Paragraph 3: By his freshman year in high school we were still navigating the same patterns. Not getting the right work done in the right order. Not understanding which assignments actually moved the grade. Not knowing that a test was coming and that preparation needed to start days in advance, especially for a brain that moves fast and gets distracted easily. That year felt different because the stakes were different. His grades could now shape his future. And he still resisted activating his support system at school, afraid of being seen as someone who could not manage on his own.
- Paragraph 4: Around the same time, my younger son was diagnosed with Oral Written Language Disorder. Two kids, two diagnoses, two IEPs. The same gap for both of them: the moment they sat down to navigate homework independently at home, the support structure that existed at school simply was not there.
- Paragraph 5: I had spent over a decade in tech. That combination of lived experience and professional instinct pointed in one direction. Maybe this was exactly the problem technology could help solve. That is how Opolo was born, built first with my own kids, shaped by their feedback from the very beginning.
- Attribution: Carol Grant, Founder
