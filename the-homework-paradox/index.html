81%

ADHD and executive function lead the sample. The survey was distributed through ADHD-focused parent communities online, so the sample skews toward ADHD and executive function challenges. Families whose child's primary diagnosis is autism, dyslexia, or a language-based learning difference without an ADHD co-diagnosis are likely underrepresented. Every finding in this report should be read with that context.

Section 05 · The gap

A plan on paper isn't support at home.

7 in 10

have a formal plan in place, and are still overwhelmed at home. Across all levels, 71% of these students have an IEP or 504 plan. The support that exists at school does not follow them home, which is exactly where the work falls apart.

Satisfaction with school homework support collapses by high school.

Satisfied (4–5 of 5) Dissatisfied (1–2 of 5)

14%

By high school, only 14% of parents are satisfied with the support their school provides, down from 29% in the earlier years. Across the whole sample, 48% are dissatisfied. Satisfaction does not hold steady as the work gets harder. It falls away.

By high school, nearly a third of families are spending $500 or more a month on outside resources like tutoring and academic coaching, almost triple the rate of middle school families. The spending peaks where school satisfaction is lowest.

29%

Middle school carries the heaviest hands-on load. The most parents spending four or more hours a week helping, yet the least likely to pay for outside help. This is a gap families have not yet found a solution for.
